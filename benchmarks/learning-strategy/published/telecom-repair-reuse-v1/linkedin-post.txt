Getting closer to fully autonomous learning, step by step…

Can an AI agent turn a successful repair into something it can do again—without reasoning through the whole problem from scratch?

We’re testing that in Bitterbot.

The agent discovered repairs while investigating failed synthetic support tasks. We verified those repairs, then asked a model to turn the evidence into conditional, executable procedures.

Two independently generated procedures each:

• Fixed 12 of 28 additional failed recorded states—43%.
• Preserved all 16 already-working states, without changing them.
• Required zero LLM calls during reuse.

Generating the procedures required model calls. Executing them didn’t.

The most interesting moment came when an agent applied a remembered repair, checked the result, saw that it hadn’t worked—and discovered an additional intervention. Removing either intervention made the failure return.

That points toward a practical learning loop:

Reuse what worked → verify the outcome → investigate what remains broken → retain the next verified repair.

The limits matter: these are development results from four previously seen synthetic cases. Executing the procedures tied ordinary advice at 6/12 on the original inputs, and the qualification gate failed. Broader transfer remains unproven.

We have a working piece of the learning loop: agent-discovered experience becoming reusable behavior, with evidence attached.
